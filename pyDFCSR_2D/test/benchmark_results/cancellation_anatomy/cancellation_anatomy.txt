----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(A2) Does C_point CONVERGE in node count?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  sum|parts| is NOT invariant to refinement in general: splitting one cell
  into two of half the weight leaves sum|.| unchanged ONLY where the
  integrand keeps its sign. Where it oscillates on the cell scale, sum|.|
  grows without bound and C_point would be measuring the mesh, not the
  physics. A converging C_point means the +/- lobes are RESOLVED structure.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(B) SCALING: C_point against L_f/sigma_z, by varying sigma_z at fixed R
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L_f = (24 R^2 sigma_z)^(1/3), so L_f/sigma_z ~ sigma_z^(-2/3). If the
  cancellation is the kernel varying across the bunch, C_point should
  follow it with slope ~1 on a log-log fit against L_f/sigma_z.

    sigma_z     L_f      L_f/sigma_z   C_point (median over the cut)
       31.7 um   156.1 mm      4923.7         52.0
       53.7 um   186.0 mm      3466.8         60.0
      101.9 um   230.4 mm      2261.2         85.3
      200.9 um   288.9 mm      1437.7        130.1

    log-log slope of C_point against L_f/sigma_z: -0.76
    A slope of +1 would mean the cancellation IS the kernel varying
    across the bunch. It is not: the sign is wrong, so C_point RISES
    as the bunch lengthens. Since L_f ~ sigma_z^(1/3), a slope of -2/3
    against L_f/sigma_z is the same as +1 against sigma_z alone, i.e.
    the cancellation is set by the bunch length in ABSOLUTE terms and
    the formation length drops out.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(D) KERNEL PROJECTION: which component of grad(rho) survives
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  numerator1 = h * K . grad'(rho) with K = v - (v.v')v' and
  grad'(rho) = rho_x n' + (rho_z/h) tau'. So drho/dx enters weighted by
  K.n' and drho/dz by K.tau'.

  ANALYTIC, not fitted. v' = tau' + vx' n' gives v'.tau' = 1 and
  v'.n' = vx', so
      K.tau' = -vx' (v.n')
      K.n'   = (v.n')(1 - vx'^2) - vx' (v.tau')
  hence K.tau'/K.n' -> -vx' whenever |vx'| << sin(phi). And since K is the
  part of v perpendicular to v', |K| = sin(phi) with phi the bend angle
  between s and s'. The table below is a numerical check of both.
