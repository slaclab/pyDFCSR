Localization branches: corrected Eq 4.24 vs the measured integrand support

NOTE on "tilt": SHEAR below is the distgen `shear z:x` coefficient applied at
s = 0. The dipole dispersion then rotates the beam in x-z, so the tilt AT THE
OBSERVATION POINT is quite different and is what actually sets the branch
geometry. Both are reported per case.

  Eq 4.24 assumes ONE beam axis. The lab-frame blend superposes two,
  so there is no single axis to feed it: the radicand goes negative
  (nan) or the root lands between the two real branches. That is not
  a failure of the formula, it is a statement that this interpolant
  has no well-defined localization geometry.

--- interval count, the direct signature of ghosting ---
  bspline_fft      : [1, 3, 3, 3, 3]   <- each snapshot contributes its own pair
  bspline_comoving : [2, 2, 2, 2, 2]   <- one blended frame, so one pair

  Eq 4.24 predicts TWO branches. The lab-frame blend produces more than
  two because it superposes two frames; the extra ones are ghosts. The
  co-moving path matching the predicted count is the ghosting fix seen
  directly in the integration plane.
